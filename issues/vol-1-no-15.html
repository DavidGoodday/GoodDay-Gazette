Finish The Job

The Whole Company Went to Austin to Finish the Job, and Came Back With Seven Demos

Three days at WeWork Barton Springs under a one-line theme. Both standing meetings went dark, the Notion record is the thinnest this paper has covered — and Thursday's wrap-up put a PO configurator, a 135,000-order sale and an agent-scoring harness on the screen in one sitting.
By the Gazette Desk · Sources: ATX Onsite wrap-up recording (Aug 20), #goodday-team, #go-to-market, #merchant-success, Notion

The FY26 Austin on-site ran Tuesday through Thursday at 801 Barton Springs Road under the theme Kyle Hency set in advance: "Finish the job." Tuesday was State of the Business and Product Vision plus an AMA, nine to two. Wednesday brought an accounting walk-through in the morning, functional working sessions for go-to-market and Merchant Success in the afternoon, and the full-team dinner at The Well Bouldin. Thursday closed with a wrap-up presentation from eleven to noon, followed — for the go-to-market team — by a three-hour pipeline cleanup that River Crawford joined remotely. That wrap-up hour is the most substantive record of the week, because it is the only part of the on-site that was recorded and shared: Meagan Herfkens Hency posted the link into #goodday-team at 10:55 on Thursday morning. What the rest of the three days left behind, or failed to, is on P.8.

Seven demos ran back to back. Seth Horton opened with a PO configurator that lets a merchant customise purchase order templates — adding and removing fields, dropping in custom content and logos — with per-merchant template defaults and sales-order customisation queued next. He had already posted a working build into #goodday-team that morning; it drew four fire reactions before the session started. Noel Klein followed with the number of the week: Lola's Black Friday event produced 135,000 orders, past what the stress tests had predicted, and held because the team put a "fast lane" in front of it to stop the infrastructure saturating. Willy Husted and others walked returns onboarding out of its corner — the implementation had been hard-coded to ShipHero, and now works against other warehouse systems, Deposco first.

The back half was about being able to see things. Scott demonstrated prefetching and client-side caching to make the app feel faster. Rob Hatfield showed a front-end monitoring dashboard that finally gives GoodDay visibility into how users navigate and how long pages actually take — which, following a week in which four separate incidents were diagnosed only after a merchant reported them, is the more consequential of the two. And Ashwin Pai closed with an evaluation harness for the AI agents: a way to score the recon agent against different question types and track whether it is getting better or worse over time. Sai Ancha keeps feeding it data. The full rundown, with chapter timings and owners, is on P.5.

Everything else in the company's formal record simply did not happen. There is no All Hands page for the week and no Voice of the Merchant page — both exist in Notion as unfilled templates, every field reading TBD (P.8). The commercial week ran in parallel: Toccin held its kickoff Tuesday, #go-to-market spent Monday scrubbing the Q3 forecast down to ten or fifteen deals, and two prospects walked — both citing finance depth (P.2). Hunter Leeming's Monday post put Troubadour Goods live and the August tally at seven go-lives. Pylon, meanwhile, took its heaviest week on record.

Two Losses, One Reason

£50 Million Was the Stated Objection. Multi-Entity Was the Real One.

Promixx passed on GoodDay because its buyer thinks the company will outgrow the product. River Crawford's read is that GoodDay already runs larger brands — and that the actual blocker is three legal entities and multi-currency. HubSpot gained a Loss Reason Detail field the same day.
Sources: #sales-support, #go-to-market, 9 Gong calls (Kelley Flatley, Kyle Hency)

Jon at Promixx wrote a rejection letter good enough to publish. "I was very impressed with this product and loved the feature of it being an app through Shopify," he told River Crawford — before the turn: "I believe that GoodDay ticks the boxes for many features which we would benefit from, but I feel that it would be limiting to when we scale in the next 3 years to £50+million revenue." River brought it back to #go-to-market on Tuesday with the diagnosis attached: "while I think he's ruling us out for the wrong reason, I'm not sure we actually want to push this one right now anyway." GoodDay runs significantly larger brands than Promixx. What GoodDay does not yet run well is three separate entities across the UK, EU and US on multiple currencies — which is exactly Promixx's shape, alongside Amazon FBA in three regions, Amazon Vendor Central, three Shopify Plus storefronts, TikTok, SPS EDI into Walmart, Meijer, GNC and Vitamin Shoppe, and three independent 3PLs. River's plan is to rebut the size objection so it does not calcify into a myth, then circle back when multi-entity ships. Meagan Herfkens Hency had Breeze log the whole thing as a note on the company record, and the same afternoon announced a new HubSpot field: "if we loose because of a feature gap, or som other reason worth noting, please take a second to fill this in so we can reference for future opportunities."

It was that kind of week for the finance roadmap. Left On Friday's deal is now openly gated on it — three-way match at end-2026, multi-entity and multi-currency in Summer 2027, Xero probably before a native ledger. Bombshell Sportswear spent sixty-six minutes with Kelley Flatley and Dave Wardell on multi-stage co-manufacturing and then pushed back on adopting a Q4 build before it reaches general availability in January or February. And Tushy, which River liked and which liked GoodDay, had already signed with Tether after coming off Extensiv. "But she really liked GoodDay!" River reported — and then extracted the consolation prize: the contact says she is well connected and asked for a one-pager she can hand to other operational leads shopping for ERP.

Monday was a forecast scrub. Kyle Hency had the team update close dates across Best Case, Most Likely and Commit and load every live deal with a 50%-or-better chance of closing before the end of October, then said the quiet part: "we'll use this list to isolate 10-15 deals that are most important and that we're going to hack at this week when we have downtime." MeUndies is the one with a clock on it — four follow-ups out of Kyle's Friday email, an Implementation Blueprint due inside two weeks, and a directive to "build a game plan to try to close them in the next 6 weeks."

Documentation

The Lot Rebalancing Spec, and "The New AI Slop"

Troy Lowe posted the finished rule set for lot rebalancing on Wednesday — redistributing available inventory within a segment to heal negative balances, moving remaining negatives to the canonical default lot, reducing duplicate default lots — worked out with Dave Wardell and filed as GD-13375. His verdict has become the week's most-quoted line: "Claude going absolutely crazy with random meaningless buzz words… I feel like this is the new AI slop, noticing it everywhere now." Troy walked James Hargett through the basics in person instead.
