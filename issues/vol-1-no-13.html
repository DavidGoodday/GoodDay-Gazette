Three Go-Lives in One Night — and the Heaviest Queue Yet

Aaron Levine, Over The Moon and Minnow all crossed on Thursday, the most GoodDay has ever launched in a single day. Underneath it: ~263 Pylon tickets, a 931-row returns failure at Poncho, and three merchants walking away from the same incumbent.
By the Gazette Desk · Reporting July 31 – August 7, 2026

Thursday, August 6, is now the busiest launch day in company history. Aaron Levine went live on Scale, Over The Moon on Bergen — both run by Seth Horton — and Minnow on IDF, run by Hunter Leeming with River Crawford doing most of the legwork. Willy Husted posted the warning to #engineering at 12:29 p.m.: "there are effectively 3 go lives this evening… Hold off on deploys rest of day unless necessary." Cloth & Paper had already crossed on Tuesday, beating its original 8/14 target. Four launches in four days, against a WMS tracker that says August should hold eleven.

None of the three went cleanly, and that is the more interesting story. Aaron Levine's 4 p.m. cutover broke immediately: the Scale integration, scoped to business unit ALV, was pulling SKUs belonging to Scale's other customers, so the account's inventory levels could not be resolved. Two hours of internal troubleshooting, an escalation to Scale, and a configuration fix from Willy later, it was live. Over The Moon — a catalog of roughly 350,000 SKUs whose first sync attempt overloaded the database — went only because Kevin Samoei finished and tested Shopify tag filtering (GD-12854) that morning. Minnow's cutover carried 88 SKUs and nearly 2,500 units of variance into the reconciliation.

The queue paid for the volume. Pylon opened roughly 263 tickets in the window, the heaviest week the Gazette has tracked, above No. 12's ~227 and No. 5's 202. Engineering shipped 15 production releases across seven days, every one flagged yellow on schema drift. And on Thursday evening Willy's investigation of a 931-row spreadsheet from Poncho Outdoors turned up 228 customers whose returns had been restocked in ShipHero five or more days earlier and never refunded (P.5).

The commercial side had a pattern of its own: three separate merchants — Fleur du Mal, Diane von Furstenberg and Ramy Brook — all moved on GoodDay in the same five days, all leaving RLM, all sitting on Bergen (P.2). Saint Art NY signed on Tuesday. And Evan Smith finally got a meeting with Intuit after roughly a year of being told GoodDay was too small (P.3).

Three Brands Leave RLM in One Week, and the Board Gets a Forecasting Religion

Fleur du Mal, DVF and Ramy Brook all move — every one of them on Bergen. Saint Art NY signs. And an outside advisor from Vanta walks the deal review through what single-threading costs.
Sources: #sales-support, #go-to-market, Gong (Flatley, Hency) · 15 calls

The week's pattern named itself. Fleur du Mal kicked off onboarding Monday, replacing RLM with Bergen Logistics on the back office — the first GoodDay merchant using Bergen's operations team rather than just its warehouse. Wednesday, Diane von Furstenberg demoed with Kelley Flatley and River Crawford, also on Bergen, also weighing whether to follow Bergen off RLM. Thursday, Ramy Brook's Jeff Green did the same. On that call Kyle Hency said GoodDay has already begun migrating clients off RLM, naming Guizio and Fleur du Mal, with a third signature expected the same day. Green's objection was not the software — he said he felt nickel-and-dimed and had lost the partnership value since the acquisition. His business is $100M-plus, factored through Hilldun, and ships exclusively by air; Kyle proposed a roughly twelve-week implementation and an in-person New York meeting inside the month.

The one clean signature was Saint Art NY, announced by River in #go-to-market Tuesday afternoon: "Saint Art NY signed!! 🎉" — followed immediately by the discovery that the customer had entered no billing details. Kyle's response set a rule for the desk: "We can email them later, but we want them to give us this as a part of signing. You have to ask and remind them through the signature process." Matt Wardell asked to be looped in for invoicing. In Tuesday's deal review, Saint Art was the only deal on the board sitting above best case.

That deal review is the other story. Kyle brought in outside sales advisor Eric Martin, three years at Vanta, and has booked him every other week through the end of September. Four themes came out: everyone preps their own deals, stage and close date and next steps go into HubSpot in advance, be explicit about commit versus most likely versus best case — and stop single-threading. Kyle admitted on the call he had never heard the concept before. Kelley's read in Slack: "Overall found meeting really helpful but I'd also like a more generic time with Eric to get more tactical tips on each part of our process." By Thursday, Meagan Herfkens Hency had shipped a forecast-category field on HubSpot deals.

Intuit Finally Answers — and One API Gap Turns Out to Block Three Partners

After roughly a year of "too small," Evan Smith gets inside Intuit Enterprise Suite. Meanwhile EnvoyB2B, NuOrder and JOOR all want the same wholesale endpoints, and Orderful is stuck behind an outside integrator.
Sources: #partnerships, Gong (Smith) · ~28 messages, 7 partner calls

The door opened Friday morning. Evan Smith's twenty-one-minute call with Zachary Williams put GoodDay in front of Intuit Enterprise Suite — the multi-entity, up-market QuickBooks tier Intuit launched about a year ago, already at $150M ARR against an ICP of $10M-plus in revenue, built expressly to stop QuickBooks customers churning to NetSuite. Two facts make it live: IES and QBO share identical API endpoints, so a QuickBooks integration is automatically an IES integration; and Intuit is not investing in the inventory side and doesn't believe it's strong enough to hold customers. Evan pitched a crawl-walk-run — referral agreement, then deep IES integration, then strategic partnership — and Williams offered a joint webinar plus technical resources, with referral program details to follow. The complication is DOSS, which Dave Wardell notes Intuit Ventures invested in heavily and is trying to make the de facto IES partner. Evan's answer: "DOSS is a shell game, we will get in there."

The week's cleanest engineering ask came out of EnvoyB2B. Tom Rau's team has scoped a four-week, two-sprint build and is waiting on GoodDay for a sandbox, company createdAt/updatedAt, a customer-address array at the Company level, filtering and sorting on updatedAt for items, and a flag to mark which products are Envoy products. Evan's own note on the thread is the reason it belongs on the front of this page: "FWIW NuOrder will want this too as it's basically handled completely manually today. JOOR got back to me yesterday the integration is finally in active scoping with them and I bet they come request this as well." One gap, three partners. The Envoy marketing launch is on hold until the build ships, and the partner agreement — still the January 1 version covering only revenue and lead sharing — is sitting in legal with Tom saying he'll sign.

Orderful remained the slowest domino. It surfaced in three separate threads from three teams: James Hargett on Friday, Kyle on Monday, and river on Wednesday flagging that TomboyX will need it by around October. The blocker isn't GoodDay engineering — it's LYSI, Orderful's integration partner, sitting on a proposal. The useful discovery came Tuesday, when Hunter Leeming noted that LYSI cofounder Jim Beavers "was a consultant for us at Chubbies for awhile." Three warm paths to the man holding the proposal, none of them used until this week.

Seth Horton ran a three-night go-live slate. Hunter cut Minnow off Cin7 with 88 SKUs of variance. And a reconciliation run at Eby wiped thousands of units of on-hand before the next morning fixed itself.
Sources: #merchant-success, #sers-tavern, Pylon, Gong (MS team) · 62 calls

Seth Horton posted the slate on Monday: Cloth & Paper Tuesday, Aaron Levine Wednesday, Over The Moon Thursday, all at 4 p.m. Eastern. Cloth & Paper landed first and best — live Tuesday, ahead of its original August 14 target, with GoodDay now source of truth for inventory while ShipHero keeps receiving and counts. Procurement moved off Inventory Planner entirely; reserves, safety stock, wholesale, DTC, subscription, COGS, QuickBooks invoicing and Amazon FBA all came with it. The merchant did it having lost the person who owned product creation across all their systems weeks before cutover, with Annie stepping in as new Head of Ops. The Channel The Team Uses To Diagnose, In One Pass. Fifty-four triage threads in seven days, and the pattern across the best of them was the same: the agent produced a fast root cause, a human pushed back, and the second or third answer was the one worth keeping.

Ten Thousand's missing two units. David Ortiz flagged a SKU showing zero on-hand in GoodDay against two in Outerspace. Ser blamed a SKU-string mismatch; David posted the screenshot proving otherwise, and Ser re-diagnosed: 40,896 delta rows, 969 with non-zero on-hand delta, and zero reconciliation outcomes — variances detected but never queued for acceptance. Willy Husted pulled the raw Trackstar payload, and David spotted the real fault: Outerspace's location number is suffixed where Trackstar expects NJ04.

DALA's vanishing feed. A ~710-item, roughly 65% coverage gap between what GoodDay tracks at Fulfillment Crowd and what the feed returns. Dave Wardell's verdict on the first answer — "You hallucinated somewhere along the way" — produced a corrected 98-SKU CSV and GD-12866, which Willy closed on Tuesday after widening the recon drop window from 30 days to 180.

American Tall's stuck receipts. The longest thread of the week, 21 replies. Seth pushed back three separate times, and each push improved the answer: two genuine duplicates created 39 milliseconds apart from the same webhook, one legitimate receipt whose allocation completed but whose status never advanced, and a merchant-level auto-receive setting behind the rest. Ser's closing line — there is no background sweeper that retroactively closes receipts with fully completed block items.

Eby's null UPCs. Three PO blocks failing to Deposco with HTTP 422. The integration maps SKU to UPC, so any item with a null UPC emits a null SKU. Matt Wardell's follow-up found the twist: the items exist, but their parent items were soft-deleted in March, so the merchant literally cannot add the UPCs through the UI.

Guizio's phantom dimensions. An import path minted four bogus dimension rows per parent, literally named "Dimension A Name" and "Dimension A Value," so chips rendered as "Dimension A Name COLOR." No UI path to fix — engineering surgery across three tables, with roughly 2,000 more SKUs queued for upload.

The Ship Log

Fifteen Releases, One Eleven-Minute Brownout, and 228 Refunds That Never Fired

Rob Hatfield takes air traffic control and walks into a full board. A 654-kilobyte webhook takes the platform down for eleven minutes. And a returns pipeline at Poncho turns out to have been quietly failing five different ways.
Sources: #engineering, #critical-support, #product-support, Linear

Rob Hatfield picked up ATC on Monday and opened with the observation that says most of what needs saying: "Follow up to my first ATC question. The Linear critical support view has quite a few tickets. 😬" He shipped or drove at least six fixes across the week — a Server Components crash on purchase orders containing an item with no parent, a Bogey Bros export failing on a Rust string conversion, a Google Workspace Marketplace geo-restriction that blocked Salt Gypsy in New Zealand, a transfer-order closure using the wrong ledger transaction type at Still Here, and a Margaux currency bug that Ser blamed on vendor-record ordering and Rob traced to the CSV importer. Two hundred–plus purchase orders were converted back to euros the same day; the permanent fix is GD-13016.

Monday's slowdown began at 7:58 a.m. Pacific when James Hargett reported the platform running very slow. The root cause landed inside the hour: a 654-kilobyte Shopify events message hit the worker at 14:51:43 UTC and held a Cloud SQL connection for 48 seconds before failing with a 500, driving the connection count from about 1,040 to 1,451. Because web-api-prod shares the instance, transfer-order fulfillment and sales-order detail queries queued behind it and p99 latency hit 4,300 milliseconds. A second burst at 15:08 exhausted worker instances and produced ten-plus 429s with a p99 of 58 seconds. The whole thing self-resolved in roughly eleven minutes when the job timed out. Dave Wardell's architectural read, two days later: state-based workflow management with replays looks safer than the current webhook handling as the platform scales.

The week's deepest hole was at Poncho Outdoors. Willy Husted spent Thursday running Temporal workflow analysis, read-replica queries and worker logs against a 931-row merchant spreadsheet and bucketed every order into a failure class. The result was nine Linear tickets filed in under thirty minutes: 228 orders restocked in ShipHero five or more days ago whose refunds never fired because GoodDay never closed the return; 110 authorized in GoodDay whose ShipHero push still failed; 34 stuck in draft; 15 parked in retryable error; and 13 true drops where the return never reached GoodDay at all — the most severe class, because those returns are invisible to the system. Dave pulled Noel and Ashwin onto it Thursday evening and approved sharing GoodDay's internal tracking sheet directly with the merchant: "Let's be VERY transparent with them about any issues and where we are at."

Scott Takes a Chainsaw to Parent Items, and a Date Column Turns Out to Be Dead

A simplification spec, a made-to-order partnership shaped, two verified dbt bugs, a POS decimal that shouldn't exist — and an Amazon warehouse question answered by finding out Amazon controls the warehouse.

Scott Meadows opened Tuesday with what he called a major simplification of parent items, arguing they are "a simple container" whose editable fields are "a hangover from the item details template." Agents & MCP

"Seems to Me Like Ser Is Getting Less Reliable Lately"

James Hargett's Tuesday question turned agent complaints into a Linear project with Willy Husted's name on it — while two agents and a senior engineer confidently agreed on a database column that was dead.
Sources: #project-ai, #mcp-launch-skills, agent threads

The question that framed the week came from James Hargett on Tuesday morning: "Seems to me like Ser is getting less reliable lately. Is there a process we should be following to surface these issues so we can address the logic behind the response. Should we create tickets and post them in this channel?" Dave Wardell's answer on Wednesday: yes, there's a Linear project, tickets are accumulating, and they get tackled in bunches once Harish is back. The project has a name and an owner — Ser V3, led by Willy Husted, covering work, bugs and improvements to the alert-triage agent. Dave then filed a ticket conversationally in-thread, asking Ser to distinguish GoodDay Sheets from the Data Warehouse from Sigma and route accordingly. Elapsed time from request to filed ticket, GD-12948: twenty-four seconds.

The failure that prompted it is instructive. James reported that the Sales Order Payment Terms tab of GoodDay Sheets had no orders after July 22. Ser's first answer, seven minutes and $1.44, was a genuinely good root-cause analysis of the wrong system — it blamed a Sigma workbook running against the dev connection, and the report in question isn't a Sigma report. James: "Why are you referencing Sigma? This is not a sigma report." The corrected answer was right and useful: the in-sheet refresh button runs the Apps Script add-on and never re-calls the GoodDay API, which is exactly why it completes in milliseconds. Ser also identified the merchant unprompted from account names visible in the screenshot, and flagged the real caveat — only sales orders with an installment schedule appear in that export at all, so of roughly 396,000 orders after July 22, just 32 qualify. Rob had it code-complete inside nineteen hours.

Then came the column that wasn't there. Asked which BigQuery field maps to a purchase-order block's ETA, Ser answered current_fulfillment_date at 95% confidence, marked verified. Dave brought in a second agent, Macroscope, which independently confirmed it from the Prisma schema through dbt into BigQuery. Troy validated it from memory. Everyone signed off Tuesday evening. On Wednesday afternoon the Eby team reported the field is always null — and Ser came back in forty-one seconds at 99% with the numbers: zero of 17,494 lines populated. Macroscope confirmed there is no application write path at all, and flagged that days_past_eta and eta_date_slipped may be wrong downstream as a result. Troy's line — "this should be one of those things you can count on it getting right, grr" — is the honest summary of the week's AI story.
