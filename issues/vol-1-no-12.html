The Ceiling, and the Fix

The Week GoodDay Outgrew Its Own Pipes

A record support load, a 200-instance Cloud Run ceiling, and a rough Wednesday tested the platform — even as the pipeline swelled to its biggest week yet and a marquee 3PL account changed hands.
By the Gazette Desk · Reporting July 23 – 30, 2026

It was the week the growth caught up with the plumbing. Overnight on July 27, scheduled jobs were silently dropped for 87 merchants — the worker returned an HTTP 200 and never retried — after GoodDay's Cloud Run services, which share a single regional pool capped at 200 instances, hit their ceiling in the morning fan-out. More than a thousand "no available instance" aborts followed between 6:38 and 7:50 a.m. UTC. Layered on top: a publishing overload with 70-plus brands pushing to Shopify overnight during recon, and a mid-week OneSchema outage that erred CSV flows. The Pylon queue swelled to roughly 227 tickets — the heaviest week the Gazette has tracked, clearing the prior high of 202 from No. 5.

The response was fast and unglamorous. Kevin Samoei's root-cause pointed at the shared instance pool and per-minute job quota; his stagger-and-skip fixes took Job-Run-per-minute quota errors to "zero this morning, down from 221 yesterday," with a proposal to raise MaxInstances 200→500 and JobRun/min 180→600 in us-central1. Go-lives were paused mid-week. By Wednesday, Hunter Leeming could report that recon "ran successfully for all 48 live merchants last night and the processing queue has returned to normal operations."

The demand side was the other half of the story. The sales board had its biggest week yet — a hydration brand projecting $75–100M, a 100,000-order-a-month underwear label weighing a NetSuite migration (P.2). And a partnership milestone landed: ShipFusion onboarded Prepared Hero, an account that had just left Stord (P.3).

A hard week, in other words, of exactly the kind a company wants: the platform strained because more merchants are asking more of it — and the team spent the week widening the pipe.

The Machine Room

The 200-Instance Ceiling — and a Hotfix Train That Ran Hot

Cloud Run's shared instance pool hits its cap, a dead-letter queue swallows 67 jobs, and a Friday-night release fixes a $433K ledger bug while breaking prod performance.
Sources: #engineering, #critical-support, #product-support

Capacity was the week's dominant story. Overnight on July 27, scheduled jobs — legacy recon and others — were silently dropped for 87 merchants: the worker returned HTTP 200, no retry fired (GD-12634). Kevin Samoei's Wednesday root-cause found that all Cloud Run services share a single regional pool capped at 200 instances, and the morning fan-out blew past it, producing over a thousand "no available instance" aborts between 6:38 and 7:50 UTC. His stagger-and-skip fixes eliminated the per-minute quota errors — "zero this morning, down from 221 yesterday" — with a proposal to raise MaxInstances 200→500 and JobRun/min 180→600. A companion failure, the worker dead-letter queue (GD-12776), permanently dropped 67 jobs across ~15 merchants after 429s exhausted the retry ceiling and landed on DLQ topics that have no subscriber.

The release train was busy and, by Thursday, bruised. v0.139.0 shipped ~84 commits and required two hand-built DB indexes — a unique index on lots and a new one on the 24-million-row integration_objects table — built in low-traffic windows. A rapid hotfix run to v0.139.5 followed; Kevin's "smarter job skipping" cut jobs launched per scheduled run from ~61 to ~12. But v0.139.5, cut Thursday, was a mixed bag: it fixed a revenue-ledger duplicate-sale race on Amazon FBA (~9,500 duplicate records inflating GMV ~$433K pre-launch), corrected ~10,700 COGS events, and closed a security hole where a merchant's API key could reach internal operator tools — now a 403. It also caused prod performance regressions, and Dave Wardell asked the evening question no one likes: "Can we roll back?"

Reliability work ran in parallel: Rob Hatfield's "thundering herd" fix caps webhook releases at 500 per merchant and 2,500 globally after a single merchant's 30k-row products/update storm overloaded MySQL, and Noel Klein staged an event-ingestion throttle to QA.
